Watering Tips

The Beginner's Guide to Watering Plants

Learn how to check soil moisture, choose the right time to water, and build a simple routine for healthier houseplants.

G The Green Corner Team
October 18, 2026 4 min read

Watering is one of the most important parts of plant care, but it is not always easy to know how much water a plant needs. Different plants, pots, and growing conditions can require different routines.

1. Check the soil before watering

Do not water a plant just because a certain number of days has passed. Instead, check the soil first. For many houseplants, feeling the top few centimetres of soil can help you decide whether watering is needed.

If the soil is still damp, wait and check again later. Plants that prefer drier soil may need longer between waterings than plants that prefer consistently moist conditions.

2. Water the soil thoroughly

When a plant needs water, water the soil evenly until some excess water drains from the bottom of the pot. This helps moisture reach more of the root area.

Empty any collected water from the outer container or saucer so the roots do not sit in standing water.

3. Use a pot with drainage holes

Drainage holes allow excess water to escape. Without proper drainage, soil can remain too wet and roots may struggle to get enough air.

Choose a pot suited to the size of your plant and use a potting mix appropriate for that plant species.

4. Understand what affects watering needs

Sunlight, temperature, humidity, pot size, soil type, and plant growth all affect how quickly soil dries. A plant in a warm, bright location may need water more often than one in a cool, shaded room.

Check your plants regularly and adjust your routine according to their actual conditions.

5. Recognize possible signs of watering problems

Yellowing leaves, wilting, and slow growth can have several causes, including watering problems. These signs alone do not always tell you whether a plant needs more or less water.

Check the soil, drainage, light, and overall condition of the plant before making changes.

Remember

There is no single watering schedule for every houseplant. Checking soil moisture is more useful than following a fixed schedule for all plants.

Final thoughts

Good watering habits start with observing your plants. Check the soil, provide suitable drainage, and adjust your routine to the plant and its environment. Over time, these simple habits can help you care for your plants with greater confidence.
