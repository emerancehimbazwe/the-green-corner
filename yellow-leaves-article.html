Why Are Your Plant Leaves Turning Yellow?

The Green Corner · 6 minute read

Yellow leaves are a common sign that a plant's growing conditions may need attention. The cause can vary, so check the soil, light, and overall condition before changing your plant-care routine.

1. Check how often you water

Too much water can leave roots without enough oxygen and may cause leaves to turn yellow. Check the soil before watering again, and make sure the pot has drainage holes.

2. Check the amount of light

Some plants struggle in very dark places, while others can develop leaf damage in strong direct sunlight. Learn your plant's light needs and move it gradually if necessary.

3. Look at the soil and pot

Soil that stays wet for a long time may drain poorly. Make sure the pot and soil are suitable for your plant, and avoid letting the roots sit in standing water.

4. Consider normal leaf aging

It is normal for some older leaves to turn yellow and fall off as a plant grows. If many leaves change color at once, investigate the growing conditions.

5. Make changes gradually

Avoid changing watering, light, and fertilizer all at once. Check the plant carefully, make one appropriate adjustment, and observe how it responds.

Final tip

Yellow leaves are a clue, not a diagnosis by themselves. Checking soil moisture, lighting, drainage, and the age of affected leaves can help you identify what your plant needs.
